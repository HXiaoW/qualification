# CellVoyager 
Single-cell RNA sequencing is a computational biology tool that can analyze high-dimensional data that are highly valued, but realistically, datasets like this contain so much information and complexity to be analyzed by a single researcher/team, thus missing many important biological information. The CellVoyager AI agent, built based on Large Language Models, can generate and interpret scRNA analysis given an environment and a manuscript text. With multiple self-critiques and self-fixation of code, CellVoyager provides outputs of results and visualization. This model is evaluated based on CellBench, a benchmark constructed from 76 published scRNA seq studies, and its results outperform GPT4o and o3 mini by up to 23% compared to the original analyses performed by the studies' authors. One important point is the limitation of assumptions: the paper indicates that CellVoyager's performance was based on the researcher's decision about which analysis to use; however, real-world research decisions might not always be the "best choice" from a reality-based perspective. Therefore, if the 76 prior studies this paper studies continuously make less optimal choices, then CellVoyager's benchmark advantages will lose weight.

# Cell Agent
Cell Agent aims to increase automation in single-cell sequencing. As there are over 1400 different tools for scRNA seq, it requires complex knowledge and expertise, also time-consuming. Therefore, introducing Cell Agent, a multi-agent LLM based on a decision-making mechanism, was able to manage from planning for the optimized analysis, including planning, coding, interpretation, debugging, and biological reasoning. The authors evaluate CellAgent on benchmark scRNA seq data with different cell types, samples, and annotations, and compare the results with human work. They claim that the work substantially improves robustness, interpretability, and execution success rate relative to a single-agent system such as GPT4. The limitation of this design would be that its self-critique system is dependent on GPT4V, which is used to judge whether the output is good enough; however, biological reasoning is complex to decide. Therefore, if GPT4V continues to make incorrect decisions, the efficiency and accuracy of the Cell Agent will go down.

# BixBench
In the current AI world with mostly single-agent models aiming to solve a single task, however, the biological research requires a series of prompting and reasoning, in which the author believes the current AIs might not be as capable. Additionally, the current benchmark evaluating AI models for bioinformatics is too simple to reflect the real bioinformatic research pipeline: they test mostly on factual recall, multiple-choice questions, or short coding problems, which can make AI systems appear more capable than they actually are in practice. Therefore, Cell Agent is created as a benchmark that includes bioinformatic tasks from reality, such as coding and debugging, data analysis, and biological reasoning. Using this benchmark, the author tested both regular LLM, coding, and biological LLM and agents on the benchmark. They claim that the current AI models are performing well on single coding tasks and fact prompting; however, the performance is still struggling in long-chain biological reasoning, interpretation, and robust workflow execution, and the author claims that the current AI systems are not yet reliable to do autonomous bioinformatic analysis alone. The most important contribution of this research is that this benchmark is heavily focused on a realistic workflow for a bioinformatic researcher, as if the benchmark scores are not able to correctly reflect the current weakness in an AI model, researchers might rely on those models more than they should, leading to insufficient or incorrect research results.

# Gene Agent
The current problem that normal LLMs often face is forming the correct biological reasoning, as science involves a large amount of interconnected knowledge. Standard AI models generate answers based on pre-trained memories and data, but they hallucinate when asked to interpret the biological reasons and the workflow behind them, and can create fabricated information. Therefore, GeneAgent is created to generate a biologically reasoned answer based on information from biological databases and sources. The AI searches for relevant information and interprets for the final answer. GeneAgent is evaluated on around 1106 gene sets, prompted with biological questions, interpretations, and hypothesis generation, and compared the performance with other standard LLMs. The author claims that GeneAgent produces more biologically accurate and less hallucinated answers as it can access external biological sources rather than being based on pre-trained memorized information. The most significant contribution of this research is its retrieval system, which can decrease hallucination from standard LLM and make research prompting more logical and reasoned; however, the weakness is that this system is still built on GPT-4, where the standard weakness of LLM still inheres, such as possible hallucination.


# Biomni
As also discussed many times above, the current LLMs for biomedical research are very limited as they are mostly test-generating from pre-trained data, and are not reliable in scientific workflows that require complex logics, coordination of tools, databases, and biological reasoning. Therefore, Biomni is created as an agent for biomedical AI built on LLM; it can connect the complete, multi-step research workflow for biomedical research. This system includes an orchestration layer that plans tasks, selects tools, retrieves information, and integrates output across different analyses. This system is evaluated on prompting biomedical research tasks involves coding, workflow generation, biological reasoning and interpreation, and interpretation. The results were compared with other standard LLMs; they claimed that Biomni performed better on realistic and long-chained biomedical tasks, as it can access external tools and reasoning steps rather than a pretrained model. The most contributed part of Biomni is its orchestration layer that improved the realistic scientific flow, so the accuracy of this model is heavily dependent on the layers making the correct decisions.


BixBench would be the paper that most threatens the other's framing. In the other four papers, they all have seen weaknesses of the current AI models, mostly around how current LLMs are used based on memorized datasets and cannot handle complex workflows in real research work, and the new agent models improve the gap between the current models and the necessity for real-world research. However, BixBench questioned the reliability and accuracy of all other LLM models by challenging the benchmark on which those models were tested. The author believes that those current evaluations are too simple and only fulfil simple needs in answering questions that all standard LLM. can do. The results suggest that models that are strong in coding or biology knowledge might still struggle in answering long-chained biological workflows that require interpretation, debugging, and multi-step reasoning. Therefore, BixBench can directly challenge for qualification for the other papers, while the other four models are simply competitors or supplements of each other.
Among the models, CellVoyager is at the top of the other systems because of its workflow-oriented and practically integrated system. As it is automating an end-to-end single cell analysis by prompting back to itself and conducting self-critique and correction, it approaches the real research environment. Compared to the  GeneAgents, CellVoyager is broader in use cases, while it focuses on the entire workflow and mimics what a real bioinformatic researcher would do, while GeneAgents only aim to resolve the hallucination by standard LLM. Although Biomni is broader than CellVoyger, CellVoyger focuses more deeply into bioinfo-specific workflow for researchers, while Biomni is trying to solve general biomedical problems for a more complex biological problem that needs reasoning for backup. CellVoyager is more similar to CellAgent in its usage, but the two models imply different fundamentals. CellAgents are a multi-agent system, and CellVoyager is an iteration of workflow, but CellAgents are more dependent on the GPT 4V system for their accuracy. With this, CellVoyager is also more likely to be challenged by BixBench, while its usage is directly targeted by BixBench, questioning the real ability of reasoning and interpretation in a long-chain workflow.
